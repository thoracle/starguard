Starguard prototype · UI reference

Cockpit Callouts

Every element on the flight screen and the nav computer, what it does, and whether it is wired to the game or only there for looks. Numbers are the current tuning values in index.html.

  • Real wired to game state or input
  • Partly real, with a catch
  • Dressing looks only, changes nothing

What is fake

  • No control is fake. The stick and all seven buttons act on the game, and every bar shows a live value.
  • The thruster-icon bar is the energy tank, not engine health. It is the fuel everything draws on. Engine damage is a separate thing with no gauge of its own.
  • System damage has no indicator on the console. Radar, comms and engine can still be knocked out. The subspace radio reports it and keeps repeating it until you dock. Damaged comms also silences every hail.
  • The talking head is mostly flavour. It tells you who is calling, but the message itself is the text on the strip, and the head hides the radar while it is up.
  • DATE is a real clock that nothing reads. It shows only on the nav computer. Warping does cost time, but the number itself never triggers anything.
  • Pure dressing: the radar sweep line, the dashboard outline and panel seams, the scanlines, the stars and planet, and the nav computer bezel.

Ranked by usefulness

Flight-screen elements, most useful first. This is a judgement from how the game is wired and from driving it with an autopilot, not from watching people play. The weapons, aft view, throttle steps and comms are new and least tested, so their places are the most likely to move. Numbers in brackets point to the flight-screen callouts below. Dressing is left out.

Cannot play without

  1. 1
    Flight stick[7]
    Every second of flight goes through it.
  2. 2
    FIRE[17]
    The only way to remove a fleet.
  3. 3
    Gunsight and lock diamond[1]
    Tells you when a shot will land. Without the lock cue you are guessing at targets a few pixels wide.
  4. 4
    SCAN and the nav computer[13]
    The whole strategic game. It is the only place you see where fleets are heading and the only way to warp.

Makes you play better

  1. 5
    Off-glass arrows[3]
    The fastest way to find a hostile that has flown past you, which is most of a dogfight.
  2. 6
    Target brackets[2]
    Raiders are tiny at range. Red brackets are how you spot them against the stars.
  3. 7
    Subspace radio and comms text[18]
    The only channel for maydays, fleet arrivals and system damage, and the only place base health shows outside the map.
  4. 8
    WEAPON selector[14]
    Missiles end a chase the lasers would drag out, and it is the only readout of rounds left as a number.
  5. 9
    Energy bar[8]
    Energy is what ends a run. It decides when you break off and go dock.
  6. 10
    Shield bar[9]
    Tells you how many more hits you can take before systems start breaking.

Useful now and then

  1. 11
    Throttle rocker[15]
    Boost closes distance and LAUNCH leaves a dock. The speed steps matter less: most fights work at the default.
  2. 12
    Weapon bar[10]
    Earns its place for the laser, where it warns the capacitor is running down. For missiles and torpedoes the selector already shows the count.
  3. 13
    Radar scope[12]
    Mostly repeats the arrows. Its own contribution is shots coming from behind and the above/below tick. Small on a phone, and now hidden whenever someone is talking.
  4. 14
    VIEW[11]
    Needed for torpedoes and for shooting a pursuer, but turning to face a target usually does the same job.
  5. 15
    Speed tape[16]
    Confirms what the dust streaks suggest. Most useful for seeing the throttle setting after a few taps.
  6. 16
    Talking head[Comms 1]
    Character and a quick read on who is calling. The words on the strip carry the information, and the head costs you the radar.
  7. 17
    Pause[6]
    Needed, but a settings control more than an instrument, which is why it sits off the dashboard.

Flight screen

Tap a number to jump to its note. Staged shot: three hostiles, one locked, missiles selected.

Comms channel

Same staged fight, with Beta Station calling a mayday. The channel is one-way: they talk, you cannot reply.

Who hails you

  • Home Control (peaked cap): mission start, each new wave, wave cleared, low energy, a station lost.
  • Beta Station (headset) and Gamma Station (visor): a mayday when a fleet reaches them, a warning below 35% hull, thanks when you destroy a fleet that was heading for them, and a greeting when you dock.
  • Raider Lead (horned alien, in red): taunts when you meet a fleet, when you kill one of theirs, and when your shields fail. Dropped if the channel is busy.

Nav computer

Opened with SCAN. The dogfight freezes while it is open, but the fleets keep moving.
